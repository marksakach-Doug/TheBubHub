The Word Key

52 cards across 4 kinds of word · Ranks are labels only

Humus — Rules of Play

A game of words that teach each other

Worlds: A world is a sheet of paper with a growing list of words and the lines that taught them. Four worlds are already on the table, each starting with four words drawn from the deck. Nobody creates new ones.

Players: 2 to 6 take turns as speaker and everyone else decodes. Sitting alone at the table is a solo game: you write a line and seal it, then read it back later, when you have half-forgotten it.

Each Turn (12 turns total):

  1. Draw 4 cards silently from the 52-card deck.
  2. Pick the Secret: Choose 1 card as the secret word; the other 3 are decoys.
  3. Pick the World: Announce which of the four worlds you are writing for. The secret word cannot already be in or struck from that world. Any decoy already in or struck from that world is swapped for a fresh draw.
  4. Write the Line: Up to 7 words teaching the secret word. You may ONLY use words in that world (with optional -s, -es, -ed, -ing, -ly endings), free words (the, a, of, in, and, is, to, not), and . or ?. You cannot use the secret word or struck words.
  5. Decode: The 4 cards are laid out face up. Every decoder points to the word they think the line was teaching.
  6. The World Keeps What Was Heard: The word most decoders pointed to joins the world (a tie joins together). If the secret word is not among them, it is struck through and never learned by that world.

Rules That Protect the Game

  • Never explain a line, even after a miss. Meaning stays in the players’ heads.
  • Nobody judges the poetry. A line only has to be understood.
  • There is no score. A miss isn’t a loss—it’s how the world grows.
